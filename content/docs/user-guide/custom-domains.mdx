Every instance with a `dns` resource gets a **platform subdomain** in the zone of its cloud target,
for example `production-acme.apps.example.com`, with a managed certificate. You can also serve it
on hostnames you own, such as `api.acme.com`.

![The Domains tab of an instance with a verified custom domain waiting for a deployment](/screenshots/domains.png)

## What the template allows

A custom domain attaches to a resource that can serve one:

- a `dns` resource whose `customDomain` is `optional` or `required` (it is `disabled` by default);
- a `web.frontend` resource (Vercel, Render and the other frontend providers).

The tenant also needs the `CUSTOM_DOMAINS` feature, and the `maxDomainsPerInstance` limit, when
set, caps how many custom domains one instance has. The platform subdomain does not count.

## Adding a domain

1. Open the instance, then the **Domains** tab, and choose **Add domain**.
2. Enter the hostname. When the instance has more than one resource that can serve it, choose one.
3. Create the records the tab shows at your DNS provider:

| Purpose | Record | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Ownership | `TXT _orkstage-challenge.<hostname>` | The token shown, starting with `orkstage-verify=` |
| Routing | `CNAME <hostname>` | The platform subdomain, or the frontend's own host |
| Certificate | `CNAME` records from the certificate authority | Shown once the certificate is requested |

OrkStage looks up the TXT record about once a minute; **Check now** looks again on the next pass.
When it finds the record, the domain is **Verified** and you get a notification. A domain whose
record does not appear within 7 days is marked **Not verified**; **Check now** gives it another 7
days.

4. **Deploy** the instance. The deployment attaches the domain to its resource and requests a
   certificate for it.

On AWS, the certificate for your hostnames comes from ACM and is validated with the CNAME records
shown under **Certificate**, which you create at your DNS provider, because the zone is yours.
Once ACM issues it, OrkStage tells you, and the next deployment adds it to the HTTPS listener of
the load balancer. Leave the validation records in place: ACM renews the certificate with them.

## Certificates and expiry

OrkStage reads the state of each certificate every few minutes: waiting for validation, issued,
expiring soon (30 days or less) or expired. The tenant's owners and admins are notified 30, 14, 7
and 1 day before a certificate expires, once each. A renewed certificate starts the countdown
again.

## Who can use a hostname

A hostname belongs to one instance on the whole platform. Adding a hostname that another instance
already has fails with `DOMAIN_TAKEN`, without saying which tenant has it. Hostnames inside the
platform zone are refused. A claim that failed verification does not hold the hostname: someone
who owns it can add it to their own instance.

## Removing a domain

Removing a custom domain takes it off the list at once. The next deployment detaches it and its
certificate. The platform subdomain goes away with its `dns` resource.

## Through the API

| Request | What it does |
|---|---|
| `GET /instances/:id/domains` | Domains with their state and the records to create |
| `POST /instances/:id/domains` | Adds `{ "hostname": "api.acme.com", "resourceId": "dns" }` (`resourceId` is optional) |
| `POST /domains/:id/verify` | Looks up the TXT record on the next pass |
| `DELETE /domains/:id` | Removes a custom domain |

Changing domains needs the `instances:write` scope.

## On simulated targets

Simulated instances use a simulated DNS. The **Create the TXT record (simulation)** button, or
`POST /instances/:id/simulate` with `{ "resourceId": "dns", "domain": { "hostname": "...",
"ownershipRecord": true } }`, creates the ownership record, so the whole flow works without a real
zone. `certificateExpiresAt` in the same object pins when the simulated certificate expires.
